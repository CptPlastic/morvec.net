Tad Morvec

I live out on the Oklahoma red dirt, in a house that's quieter than the wind. There's a west window in the kitchen I watch more than I mean to, and a shop out back I disappear into for hours.

I was born in '62. I'll mention it once.

Most mornings the coffee goes in the thermos darker than is polite, and Nell takes the passenger seat without asking. She's a heeler mix, older now, with more opinions than teeth. We take the dirt road while the air is still cool. Doors off, if the day is going to allow it. If another woman lifts a hand out there, I lift one back. That's usually the best part, and it doesn't take long.

By the time we get home the west side of the house is just starting to think about the afternoon. I put the radio on in the kitchen and go out to the bench. I like making things with my hands. Always have. Vinyl, mostly — the stubborn kind of work where a line has to sit true or I start over. A few of those pieces live on Etsy, if you ever wander over.

I used to be an engineer, technically. Quiet work. Getting a system to line up. I still can't leave a thing that sits crooked. Same hands. Different material.

The light in November gets long and a little copper. I try to be in it.
